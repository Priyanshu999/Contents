## Research Report: Perceptual Preprocessing for Video Codecs

### Executive Summary

Perceptual preprocessing is a crucial area in video coding aimed at maximizing compression efficiency while preserving or enhancing the perceived visual quality. This report synthesizes recent advancements in the field, focusing on techniques that leverage Human Visual System (HVS) models, perceptual quality metrics, and deep learning. Key challenges include balancing compression rate with perceptual quality, adapting to diverse video content, and minimizing computational complexity. Emerging opportunities lie in novel deep learning architectures, adaptive preprocessing strategies, and the integration of perceptual metrics directly into the encoding process. This survey identifies research gaps and proposes innovative directions for future work, emphasizing the potential of hybrid approaches and real-time implementations.

### 1. Introduction

#### Slide 1: Title: Perceptual Preprocessing for Video Codecs: A Concise Survey

*   Objective: To provide a comprehensive overview of recent advances in perceptual preprocessing techniques for video coding.
*   Scope: Focus on HVS modeling, perceptual quality metrics, deep learning approaches, and adaptive preprocessing.
*   Target Audience: Researchers and engineers in video compression and image processing.

#### Slide 2: Problem Definition and Motivation

*   Problem: Traditional video codecs optimize for distortion metrics (e.g., PSNR) that do not always correlate with human perception [CVPR Poster Perceptual Video Compression with Neural Wrapping](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/poster/34427).
*   Motivation: To achieve better rate-quality performance by incorporating perceptual considerations into the video encoding pipeline [CVPR Poster Perceptual Video Compression with Neural Wrapping](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/poster/34427).
*   Benefits: Improved subjective visual quality, reduced bandwidth requirements, and enhanced user experience [Perceptual Quality: Measure & Optimize Video and Audio - FastPix](https://www.fastpix.io/blog/perceptual-quality-how-to-measure-and-optimize-visual-audio-fidelity).

#### Slide 3: Key Challenges in Perceptual Preprocessing

*   Adapting to Diverse Video Content: Developing preprocessing techniques that are robust across different video characteristics (e.g., resolution, frame rate, motion) [Hybrid Image Resolution Quality Metric (HIRQM) - arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02001v1).
*   Minimizing Computational Complexity: Ensuring that preprocessing steps do not introduce excessive computational overhead, especially for real-time applications.

*   Subjectivity in Quality Assessment: Aligning objective metrics with subjective human evaluations.

### 2. Technical Background

#### Slide 4: Human Visual System (HVS) Principles

*   Contrast Sensitivity: The HVS is more sensitive to changes in luminance than chrominance.
*   Spatial Frequency Sensitivity: The HVS is more sensitive to certain spatial frequencies than others.
*   Masking Effects: The visibility of one image component can be reduced by the presence of another component.
*   Attention Mechanisms: The HVS selectively attends to certain regions of the image based on saliency and context.
*   These principles are crucial for designing effective perceptual preprocessing techniques [Blind HDR image quality assessment based on aggregating ...](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-94005-1).

#### Slide 5: HVS Modeling Techniques

*   Contrast Masking Models: Simulate the masking effect of high-contrast regions on nearby low-contrast regions.
*   Frequency-Weighted Models: Weight different spatial frequencies based on their perceptual importance.
*   Saliency Detection Models: Identify salient regions in the image that attract human attention.
*   HDR and WCG Considerations: Account for the extended dynamic and color ranges in HDR and Wide Color Gamut (WCG) content.

#### Slide 6: Key Perceptual Quality Metrics

*   **SSIM (Structural Similarity Index)**: Measures the similarity between two images based on luminance, contrast, and structure [Wang, Z., Bovik, A. C., Sheikh, H. R., & Simoncelli, E. P. (2004). Image quality assessment: From error visibility to structural similarity. IEEE Transactions on Image Processing, 13(4), 600–612](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02001v1#bib.bib1).
*   **MS-SSIM (Multi-Scale SSIM)**: An extension of SSIM that considers multiple scales of the image.
*   **VMAF (Video Multi-Method Assessment Fusion)**: A full-reference perceptual metric developed by Netflix that combines multiple quality measures.
*   **PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio)**: A traditional distortion metric that measures the ratio between the maximum possible power of a signal and the power of corrupting noise. Often used, but does not correlate well with perceived quality [CVPR Poster Perceptual Video Compression with Neural Wrapping](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/poster/34427).
*   **AVQT**: Another objective quality score used in video compression [CVPR Poster Perceptual Video Compression with Neural Wrapping](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/poster/34427).

#### Slide 7: Objective vs. Subjective Quality Assessment

*   Objective Metrics: Automated metrics that provide a quantitative measure of video quality (e.g., PSNR, SSIM, VMAF) [Liu, Y.; Tian, Y.; Wang, S.; Zhang, X.; Kwong, S. Overview of High-Dynamic-Range Image Quality Assessment. J. Imaging **2024**, _10_, 243](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/10/5339).
*   Subjective Testing: Human evaluation of video quality using standardized methodologies (e.g., ITU-T P.910) [CVPR Poster Perceptual Video Compression with Neural Wrapping](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/poster/34427).
*   Challenge: Bridging the gap between objective metrics and subjective perception [Perceptual Quality: Measure & Optimize Video and Audio - FastPix](https://www.fastpix.io/blog/perceptual-quality-how-to-measure-and-optimize-visual-audio-fidelity).

### 3. Literature Analysis

#### Slide 8: Categorization of Approaches

*   **Adaptive Filtering**: Adjusting filter parameters based on video content characteristics [Adaptive Perceptual Preprocessing for Video Coding](https://idm.pku.edu.cn/__local/D/E3/9F/85C68F6CF34461030A9F405B6FC_2631FB10_89D9E.pdf?e=.pdf).

*   **Deep Learning-Based Preprocessing**: Using neural networks to learn optimal preprocessing strategies [A Review Toward Deep Learning for High Dynamic Range ... - MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/10/5339).
*   **Rate-Perception Optimization**: Jointly optimizing rate and perceptual quality metrics during encoding [Rate-Perception Optimized Preprocessing for Video Coding](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.10455).

#### Slide 9: Paper Analysis - Rate-Perception Optimized Preprocessing for Video Coding

*   Methodology: Proposes a rate-perception optimized preprocessing framework that jointly optimizes rate and perceptual quality.
*   Key Results: Achieves significant BD-rate savings compared to traditional codecs.
*   Strengths: Integrates perceptual metrics directly into the optimization process.
*   Limitations: High computational complexity.

#### Slide 10: Paper Analysis - Deep Perceptual Preprocessing for Video Coding

*   Methodology: Uses deep neural networks to learn optimal preprocessing strategies for video coding.
*   Key Results: Demonstrates improved rate-distortion performance compared to traditional preprocessing techniques.
*   Strengths: Captures complex relationships between video content and perceptual quality.
*   Limitations: Requires large training datasets and is computationally intensive [A Review Toward Deep Learning for High Dynamic Range ... - MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/10/5339).

#### Slide 11: Paper Analysis - New Adaptive Filters as Perceptual Preprocessing for Rate-Quality Performance Optimization of Video Coding

*   Methodology: Introduces new adaptive filters that adjust their parameters based on video content characteristics.
*   Key Results: Achieves improved rate-quality performance compared to fixed filters.
*   Strengths: Low computational complexity and easy to implement.
*   Limitations: Limited ability to capture complex perceptual phenomena.

#### Slide 12: Paper Analysis - Deformation Aware Image Compression

*   Methodology: Develops an image compression method that accounts for geometric deformations in the image.
*   Key Results: Improves compression efficiency for images with significant deformations.
*   Strengths: Robust to geometric distortions.
*   Limitations: Not directly applicable to video coding.

#### Slide 13: Paper Analysis - Saliency-Guided Video Codec Pre-Processing

*   Methodology: Uses saliency detection to guide the encoding process, prioritizing the preservation of salient regions.
*   Key Results: Improves subjective visual quality by allocating more bits to salient regions.
*   Strengths: Aligns with human visual attention mechanisms.
*   Limitations: Saliency detection can be computationally expensive.

#### Slide 14: Paper Analysis - Perceptual Prefiltering for Video Coding

*   Methodology: Applies perceptual prefiltering to reduce irrelevant information before encoding.
*   Key Results: Achieves improved rate-distortion performance compared to no prefiltering.
*   Strengths: Simple and effective.
*   Limitations: Limited ability to adapt to diverse video content.

#### Slide 15: Paper Analysis - Adaptive Perceptual Preprocessing for Video Coding

*   Methodology: Adapts the preprocessing strategy based on video content characteristics and encoding parameters.
*   Key Results: Achieves improved rate-quality performance compared to fixed preprocessing strategies.
*   Strengths: Flexible and adaptable.
*   Limitations: Requires careful design and optimization.

### 4. Research Gaps & Future Work

#### Slide 18: Identified Limitations and Gaps

*   Limited Generalization: Many existing techniques are tailored to specific video content or codecs.
*   High Computational Complexity: Deep learning-based methods can be computationally expensive.
*   Lack of Real-Time Implementations: Few techniques are suitable for real-time video encoding.
*   Subjectivity in Quality Assessment: Aligning objective metrics with subjective human evaluations remains a challenge [Perceptual Quality: Measure & Optimize Video and Audio - FastPix](https://www.fastpix.io/blog/perceptual-quality-how-to-measure-and-optimize-visual-audio-fidelity).
*   HDR Video Processing: More research is needed on perceptual preprocessing for HDR video.

#### Slide 19: Emerging Opportunities

*   Novel Deep Learning Architectures: Exploring new neural network architectures for perceptual preprocessing, such as transformers and GANs [Artificial Intelligence in Creative Industries: Advances Prior to 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02725v3).
*   Adaptive Preprocessing Strategies: Developing more sophisticated adaptive preprocessing strategies that can dynamically adjust to video content and encoding parameters [Adaptive Perceptual Preprocessing for Video Coding](https://idm.pku.edu.cn/__local/D/E3/9F/85C68F6CF34461030A9F405B6FC_2631FB10_89D9E.pdf?e=.pdf).
*   Integration of Perceptual Metrics: Integrating perceptual metrics directly into the encoding process to guide rate allocation and distortion minimization [Rate-Perception Optimized Preprocessing for Video Coding](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.10455).
*   Real-Time Implementations: Developing efficient algorithms and hardware architectures for real-time perceptual preprocessing.
*   Perceptual Preprocessing for HDR Video: Addressing the specific challenges of HDR video coding, such as tone mapping and color gamut mapping [A Review Toward Deep Learning for High Dynamic Range ... - MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/10/5339).

#### Slide 20: Future Work

*   Hybrid Metrics: HIRQM unifies statistical (PDF), multi-scale (MFS), and deep learning-based (HDIF) approaches into a single hybrid metric, offering a comprehensive solution for image quality assessment [Hybrid Image Resolution Quality Metric (HIRQM) - arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02001v1).
*   Expand HIRQM: Future advancements will focus on enhancing computational efficiency and extending its capabilities to video quality assessment, leveraging HIRQM’s robust foundation to push the boundaries of visual quality evaluation [Hybrid Image Resolution Quality Metric (HIRQM) - arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02001v1).
*   Dynamic Weighting: The introduction of an adaptive weighting scheme tailors the metric to the reference image’s properties, improving flexibility and alignment with human perception [Hybrid Image Resolution Quality Metric (HIRQM) - arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02001v1).

### Technical Deep Dive: Hybrid Image Resolution Quality Metric (HIRQM)

The Hybrid Image Resolution Quality Metric (HIRQM) represents a significant advancement in image quality assessment (IQA) by integrating multiple methods to mirror the intricacies of human vision [Hybrid Image Resolution Quality Metric (HIRQM) - arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02001v1). HIRQM unifies statistical (PDF), multi-scale (MFS), and deep learning-based (HDIF) approaches into a single hybrid metric, offering a comprehensive solution for image quality assessment.

| Module | Description |
|---|---|
| Probability Density Function (PDF) | Uses a patch size of 32x32 pixels to capture local detail.  Employs 256 bins for histograms, corresponding to the 8-bit grayscale intensity range, providing detailed granularity while avoiding excessive computational cost. |
| Multi-Scale Feature Similarity (MFS) | Uses 4 pyramid levels, enabling analysis across multiple scales to capture both fine details and broader structural features. |
| Hierarchical Deep Image Features (HDIF) | Features are extracted from layers 3, 8, 15, 22, and 29 of the VGG16 network. These layers span low-level edge detection to high-level semantic understanding. |
| Dynamic Weighting | HIRQM avoids fixed thresholds, instead using a dynamic weighting mechanism that adapts to the reference image’s properties, enhancing robustness across diverse image types. |
